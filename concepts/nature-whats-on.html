Nature as what’s on

Fourteen nature signals, checked against real sources on 31 Aug 2026. Most of them are free, and one of the two endpoints that answers six at once is already being called on every page load. Every number here says where it came from; every interface is dashed, because none of it is built.

The vocabulary is already written 7 of 14 wired up

This is not an idea to invent. Seven condition tags evaluate and seven have never done anything — met() returns null for a tag it does not know and suits() filters nulls out, so an unwired tag is silently inert. No error, no gap on screen, nothing to tell it from a row with no conditions at all.

The tide is in the marine call and it is a real lunar tide

This is the one everybody assumes needs a paid API. It does not — sea_level_height_msl comes back hourly in the same free call as the swell. But it had to be checked before it could be believed, because a sea-level field could as easily be storm surge as tide.

midnight6amnoonnowmidnight6amnoon6pm

The check: 23 high waters across a twelve-day window, mean interval between them 12.32 hours. The lunar semidiurnal M2 constituent is 12.42 h; a solar cycle or a model artefact would sit at 12.00. Hourly sampling accounts for the difference. The range also grows from 1.5 m to 2.37 m across the window, which is the spring–neap cycle turning up on its own. It is a tide.
What it is not: a gauge, and not a BOM tide table. It is an hourly model field, so the time of high water is good to about half an hour. That is fine for “low tide around 7pm” on a rockpool listing and not fine for printing a tide time to the minute. The site should say the first and never the second.

The sky three sources, three different kinds

Worth separating, because they cost completely different things. One is arithmetic, one is a live feed, one is a table somebody writes once a year.

Moon
Computed — no API at all

Phase, rise and set are deterministic astronomy, the same class of maths as the sunset this page already computes in-house. About twenty lines, no network, no third party, nothing that can go down. It wires full-moon and new-moon — and a new moon is the gate on every dark-sky listing, which is a better use of it than the bonfire.

Aurora
Live feed — free, no key

NOAA’s Space Weather Prediction Center publishes the planetary K index as JSON on a one-minute cadence. Kp is 3 right now; the aurora australis needs roughly 6 or more to be worth a drive to a south-facing beach. That is the whole of geomagnetic-storm, a tag currently on one row.

Meteor showers
A fixed calendar — written once

Peak dates barely move: the Geminids are mid-December every year, the Eta Aquariids early May. It is a table of about a dozen rows from the International Meteor Organization, not a feed. And it is the clearest case of the shape this whole page keeps running into — a dated event with no organiser, no venue and no ticket.

Fire is a season, not a day five months the site cannot name

The board knows about dates and it knows nothing about the year. These two bands are the answer to redefining what’s on: neither has an organiser, a venue or a ticket, and both change what the place is for, for months at a time.

The orchid curve is real: research-grade Orchidaceae observations by month of year from api.inaturalist.org, in a box over the Anglesea heath and the coast Torquay–Lorne. Jan 164 · Aug 949 · Sep 2,339 · Oct 2,563 · Nov 470. All plants in the same box, 28,029 records, make the same shape.
The honest caveat, which belongs on the page if this is ever printed: observation counts measure observers as much as flowers — ANGAIR’s show brings people out in September. But orchids swing 25× between trough and peak where all plants swing 9×, off the same walkers. That difference is the flowering signal separating itself from the effort.
The fire band is drawn from a typical declaration and is the one shape on this page nobody has verified — CFA’s dates table is drawn client-side, so the start date is not readable. The live state is.

Where the method breaks and why that is worth drawing

The wildflower and fungi curves work because the organism’s visibility IS the phenomenon — a flower is only recorded when it is out. Animals decouple the two, and the same query then returns a confident, well-shaped, wrong answer.

Hooded Plover

642 records · iNaturalist · peaks February
JFMAMJJASOND

The curve peaks in February and the nesting season starts in August. February is when people are on the beach. The fact that actually matters — chicks on the sand, dogs on leads — is invisible in this data and would be contradicted by it. It comes from BirdLife’s beach-nesting birds program, written down by a person. Point Roadknight already carries the dog restriction in prose.

Whales

59 records across twelve months of the year

Southern Right and Humpback pass this coast June to October and every local knows it. iNaturalist holds 59 cetacean records for the whole region — four a month. A curve off that is decoration.

The authoritative Victorian source exists and says no. WhaleFace, run by DEECA with SWIFFT and Federation University, collects exactly these sightings — and its robots.txt carries Disallow: /api/. So the whale season is a fact a person writes down, and the site links WhaleFace rather than reading it.

Two more land in the same place, for completely different reasons.

Shark sightings

Victoria publishes no feed

NSW has SharkSmart with a real API; it is the wrong state and it sits behind Cloudflare. In Victoria a sighting closes a beach through Life Saving Victoria, whose site returns 403 to anything that is not a browser.

And the honest answer is that this one should not be automated even if it could be. A shark sighting is an emergency-services message with a lifespan measured in hours. A listings site republishing a stale one is worse than a listings site carrying none — the same reasoning that keeps invented dates out of this database.

Fishing

VFA behind Cloudflare · data.vic has one habitat dataset

Closed seasons, bag limits and the runs that actually matter — snapper into the bay from October, whiting, salmon, calamari — are published by the Victorian Fisheries Authority as prose and PDF, with nothing machine-readable behind them.

Which puts fishing in the same box as meteor showers and the whale season: a fixed annual calendar somebody writes once and checks each year. That is not a failure. Three of the best things on this page are that shape.

Five kinds of thing, not one and the cost is completely different

The useful result of the whole exercise is that “nature data” is not one category. It is five, they need five different builds, and only the first is a scraping problem — which is where all the effort would have gone by default.

1 · A live feed

Swell, tide, wind, rain, sea temperature, UV, Kp, fire ban. Two Open-Meteo calls plus two free government feeds, no keys anywhere. This is nearly everything and it is nearly free.

2 · Arithmetic

Moon phase, moonrise, dark-sky windows. No network at all, and the site already does this class of maths for sunset. Cannot break, cannot rate-limit, cannot change its licence.

3 · Measured from records

Wildflowers and fungi, from iNaturalist. Works where the organism’s visibility is the phenomenon. Two seasons, opposite ends of the year, both with real curves behind them.

4 · A calendar somebody writes

Meteor showers, whale season, fishing runs, hooded plover nesting, the fire danger period’s dates. Not a failure of automation — a dozen rows, checked once a year, and three of the best things on this page.

5 · Deliberately not carried

Shark sightings. Available nowhere public in Victoria, and it would be the wrong thing to carry even if it were: hours-old safety information on a listings page is worse than none.

And the hole under all of it

It is fire danger period. The orchids are out. The falls are running. Low tide is at seven. No organiser, no venue, no ticket — and no row shape that holds any of them. That is a schema question, and it is the one to answer first.

Three things found along the way that are bugs in the database rather than ideas for it, and can be fixed today: three waterfall walks are gated on dry-trails, so they are hidden in exactly the weather that makes them worth visiting; Beauchamp Falls carries good-in-rain and dry-trails at once; and good-in-rain is doing two jobs — “pleasant in the wet” and “runs after rain” — which is why it ended up on six waterfalls and 217 other things.
