Nature as what’s on

Two of the coast’s real seasons, drawn as they would look on the board. Fire is a season that closes things. Wildflowers are a season that opens them. Every number here was fetched on 31 Aug 2026 and says where it came from; every interface is dashed, because none of it is built.

The year, as the coast actually has it two seasons nothing on the site names

The board knows about dates and it knows nothing about the year. These two bands are the answer to redefining what’s on: neither has an organiser, a venue or a ticket, and both change what the place is for, for months at a time.

The orchid curve is real: research-grade Orchidaceae observations by month of year from api.inaturalist.org, in a box over the Anglesea heath and the coast Torquay–Lorne. Jan 164 · Aug 949 · Sep 2,339 · Oct 2,563 · Nov 470. All plants in the same box, 28,029 records, make the same shape.
The honest caveat, which belongs on the page if this is ever printed: observation counts measure observers as much as flowers — ANGAIR’s show brings people out in September. But orchids swing 25× between trough and peak where all plants swing 9×, off the same walkers. That difference is the flowering signal separating itself from the effort.
The fire band is drawn from a typical declaration and is the one shape on this page nobody has verified — CFA’s dates table is drawn client-side, so the start date is not readable. The live state is.

The day a fact, not a filter

A Total Fire Ban is the single most legitimate “what’s on today” this coast has. It belongs where the date and the sunset already are — and it has to name its district, because a reader in Apollo Bay is on a different feed from a reader in Torquay.

One region, two districts the thing that decides the build

What this leaves open decide before building

Both halves came out the same way. The interesting nature data is not a filter — it is a season and a day. Seven tags have sat inert for months and nobody missed them, because hiding things is not what anyone opens a listings site to get.

The licensed feed cannot say no

The RSS is readable and CFA licenses it “personal, non-commercial use only”, pointing websites at EMV’s developer feeds instead. Those return a single newline byte — 0a — not []. It cannot tell “no bans in Victoria” from “endpoint moved”. Nobody has seen it populated; November is the first honest chance. An email to EMV settles it.

ANGAIR is a listing and not a source

No places row, robots fully open, a live calendar of walks and working bees nobody reads. But it runs Modern Events Calendar, and both halves of the GMBC trap were re-confirmed against it: mec/v1/events returns [], and wp/v2/mec-events gives the post date — all three events read back 29 Aug, the day somebody typed them in.

There is nowhere to put a season

It is fire danger period. The orchids are out. No organiser, no venue, no ticket, and no row shape that holds them. The same gap already recorded for “an organiser worth watching that is not a room”. That is the thing to decide, and it is a schema question, not a design one.
