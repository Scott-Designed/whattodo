Running

9 things on the board — three of them every week, six with a date on them — plus 4 routes and the one shop.

There is enough trail here for a hundred-kilometre race, and the Surf Coast Century is exactly that — out of Anglesea, below the sea cliffs, along remote beach, and back through the wildflower heathland.

The heath is the part that surprises people. Over a hundred species of orchid bloom between Anglesea and Aireys Inlet in spring, on ground internationally significant for its biodiversity, which ANGAIR have been protecting since 1969. Inland it turns to forest: the Point Addis circuit drops into ironbark and climbs back onto clifftop heathland above Jarosite Headland, and further west the trails run up into Great Otway National Park, where forty of them are mapped around Lorne alone.

None of which would matter without the people who turn out on it. Two free parkruns anchor the Saturday, one on the coast and one inland. A shop run leaves Ocean Grove at half past seven. And twice a year the whole coast turns out properly — Anglesea in September, Lorne in May.

Assembled from Surf Coast Century, Anglesea Heath, ANGAIR Working Bees, Point Addis – Ironbark Basin Circuit and Lorne & Otways Trails — nothing here is a new claim.
