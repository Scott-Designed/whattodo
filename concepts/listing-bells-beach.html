The story of it FROM WIKIPEDIA

Named after William Bell, a Geelong businessman and grazier who owned much of the property from the 1840s. Many records wrongly credit the name to John Calvert Bell, whose family took up a pastoral run here much later, in 1905.

As early as 1939 surfers from Torquay made their way to Bells, but access was a considerable problem until 1960, when Torquay surfers and Olympic wrestler Joe Sweeney hired a bulldozer and cleared a road along the cliff.

The final scene of Point Break is set at Bells — but was not filmed here. Bells is a straight stretch; the beach in the film is a cove, shot at Indian Beach in Oregon.

This is the best case, and it should be read as one. Bells Beach has a Wikipedia article, a heritage listing, five events and 7,147 occurrence records. Only 34 of 522 pinned listings have the article, and only 30 can show events. What every pin does have is the ecology — 18 of 18 sampled had records — so the bottom half of this page is the part that generalises and the top half is not.

The plants are the better half, and they were nearly missed. The animal section is striking but static; the plant section carries a reason to come in October, which is the only thing on this page that would make somebody change their plans. 727 orchid records within 5 km and 303 of them in one month is not a decoration, it is the listing’s single best argument for itself — and it is derived, so it costs nobody any writing.

iNaturalist is already inside the Atlas — 1,360 of the 7,147 records here are theirs, the third-largest supplier after the Victorian Biodiversity Atlas and BirdLife. So it is not a second source of facts. What it adds is photographs: 274 research-grade plant taxa within 3 km, 76 of them with a CC0 or CC BY image, which is where all five pictures above came from. Use ALA for the numbers and iNaturalist for the pictures.

Two field traps, both silent, both hit while building this. ALA’s species field is species, not taxon_name — the wrong one returns zero records and no error. Its month facet returns month names, not numbers, so an int(label) parse yields an empty chart that looks like a species with no season. On iNaturalist the same shape: taxon_name=Orchidaceae returns 0 and taxon_id=47217 returns 22.

What is NOT on this page, deliberately. No native-versus-introduced split: establishmentMeans is “not supplied” on 1,745 of the 1,990 plant records, so any percentage would be an artefact of what the recorder happened to type. And no raw record counts anywhere — 7,147 is a fact about surveyors, not about Bells Beach.
