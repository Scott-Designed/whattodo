Nature’s Noticeboard · 100 things the weather decides

What this is

A hundred natural events on the Surf Coast, drawn on the site’s own board — index.html’s stylesheet and icon sprite, lifted rather than imitated. The six columns, the 57px row, the group tint, the ON NOW pill, the pin and the icon gutter are all the real thing. What is invented is the hundred rows.

Sorted soonest first, which is the board’s default, so the things waiting on weather fall to the bottom by themselves. 9 are on right now, 5 are close, and 12 are dimmed because a condition has not been met — dimmed and not hidden, because a reader wants to know the waterfall is low rather than find it missing.

Every place is a real row in the database where one exists — 59 of them. The dates are where they genuinely fall: the Geminids on 14 December, the fire danger period from late November, fungi peaking in May, orchids in October, shearwaters back around 22 September. Four rows were cut for being out of region — Logans Beach, Portsea, St Kilda, Port Fairy — because a Perth comedy gig on the board is this project’s own standing example of that mistake.

Six of the types already exist: beach, cycling, nature, paddling, swimming, water. Fifteen do not, and adding one is five places each — fire fishing fungi hang gliding kitesurfing moon paragliding stars sunrise sunset surf tide waterfall wildflower wildlife.

And the When column is doing something it cannot do today. “Needs a southerly”, “When it lands”, “Unpredictable”, “Low tide only” are not dates and not recurrences — they are conditions, and a third of these rows have nothing else. That is the schema question, made visible rather than argued.
